# Brand & Style Guide

## Brand position
Goth Girls Comics & Collectables should feel like a real independent comic shop for serious fans and collectors, not a generic toy store or novelty superhero site.

Primary audience: adult comic and collectible fans, especially men approximately 36–55 who grew up with comics, action figures, anime, cartoons, trading cards, video games, and pop culture.

The site should remain welcoming to younger fans, families, and new collectors.

## Brand personality
- Knowledgeable
- Enthusiastic
- Slightly irreverent
- Nostalgic
- Collector-driven
- Welcoming
- Local
- Independent
- A little weird
- Passionate about fandom

## Visual formula
- 70% serious modern comic and collectibles store
- 20% vintage comic / print culture
- 10% goth and alternative personality

## Visual mood
Imagine a great independent comic shop crossed with vintage comic printing, underground pop culture, old horror comics, record-store culture, anime fandom, and a modern specialty retailer.

Curated, not corporate.

## Color direction
Primary:
- Near black
- Charcoal
- Warm off-white / aged paper

Secondary:
- Deep purple
- Dark crimson
- Muted burgundy

Accent colors may include, sparingly:
- Acid green
- Electric blue
- Yellow
- Magenta

Avoid covering the entire site in bright primary comic-book colors.

## Typography
### Headlines
Bold display type with personality. Inspiration can come from vintage comic mastheads, horror titles, punk posters, or alternative comics, but readability comes first.

### Body copy
Highly legible contemporary sans-serif.

### Navigation
Strong, clean, bold, and simple.

Avoid novelty comic fonts for body copy.

## Graphic language
Subtle references to:
- Halftone printing
- Comic panels
- Ink texture
- Newsprint
- Ben-Day dots
- Speech bubbles
- Caption boxes
- Vintage price stickers
- Comic issue numbering
- Collector labels

These are accents, not the dominant UI.

## Product imagery
Keep product photography clean and relatively neutral. Avoid placing heavy visual effects over product images.

Use stronger graphic treatments around:
- Hero banners
- Promotional sections
- Events
- Category headers
- Announcements
- Limited drops

## Product badges
Reusable labels:
- NEW
- PREORDER
- RARE
- LIMITED
- STAFF PICK
- JUST IN
- SOLD OUT

## Photography direction
Use a mix of:
- Clean product photography
- Store photography
- Shelves packed with comics and collectibles
- Close-ups of comic covers
- Figures and statues
- Trading cards
- Event photography
- Customers browsing

Photography should feel real and lived-in rather than generic stock.

## Tone of voice
Write like knowledgeable comic-shop employees talking to other fans.

Good examples:
- Just landed.
- On the shelf this week.
- Preorders close Friday.
- One for the collection.
- You probably had this in 1994.
- Rare find.

Avoid corporate phrases such as:
- Discover our curated assortment.
- Elevate your collection.
- Explore premium merchandise.

## Goth influence
Express the goth element primarily through:
- Dark palette
- Typography
- Illustration
- Occasional horror-comic influence
- Slightly macabre humor
- Alternative culture

Do not turn the site into gothic fashion branding. The word “Girls” in the store name should not make the design pink, beauty-oriented, overly cute, or overly feminine.

## Guardrails
- Do not make the site childish
- Do not make it look like a generic superhero site
- Do not bury ecommerce under decorative effects
- Do not use AI-generated images for actual products being sold
- Do not sacrifice readability for theme
